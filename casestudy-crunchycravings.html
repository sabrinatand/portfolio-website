Overview

What is Crunchy Cravings?

A startup founded by two brothers making handcrafted Middle Eastern lavosh crackers. Their goal was to go premium: fine dining events, gift hampers, wholesale partners. But when we met them, they had no digital presence at all. Everything ran on in-person sales and manual outreach.

Our team Skellix was brought in to build a two-part solution: a branded ecommerce storefront for retail customers, and an admin dashboard to manage orders and wholesale enquiries. I led the project as BA and Team Coordinator, and owned the Figma prototype and frontend implementation.

The Challenge

A premium brand with no digital presence.

The client had a great product but zero infrastructure. No way for customers to order online, no system for wholesale buyers to submit enquiries, and no central place for the founders to track anything. On top of that, the brand had a very specific identity: artisan, premium, Middle Eastern-inspired. The digital solution had to feel like an extension of that.

The trickiest part from a BA perspective was scoping realistically. The client came in with big ideas including a chatbot and AI product recommendations. Part of my job was prioritising what was achievable in two iterations and clearly documenting what was future state.

What I Did

From first meeting to final handover

01
Business Vision
Met with the client brothers to understand their goals and current pain points. Wrote the Business Vision covering the brand background, system requirements across 3 iterations, agile approach, timeline, and team structure. The client signed off before anything was built.
02
Personas
Built personas for 3 user types based on the client's real customer base. Bethany (retail customer) cared about a clean checkout. Ralph (wholesale buyer) needed a dedicated enquiry system. Sam (co-founder/admin) needed a centralised dashboard. These drove every design decision.
03
Diagrams and Modelling
Produced the full set: ER diagram, user journey maps for all 3 personas, use case diagrams, and a functional decomposition. These became the shared reference for the team and fed directly into the database schema.
04
User Stories and Acceptance Criteria
Wrote stories for all 3 roles across both iterations with acceptance criteria used as the definition of done in dev and tested directly by the client in UAT.
05
Figma Prototype and Screen Mockups
Designed the full storefront, product pages, cart, checkout, wholesale enquiry flow, and admin dashboard in Figma. Grounded everything in the premium brand identity and iterated based on client feedback.
06
Iteration Reports and UAT
Wrote iteration reports after each sprint. Prepared and ran UAT sessions with the client using the acceptance criteria, documented outcomes, and fed findings back into the next cycle.
07
Frontend Implementation
Implemented the Figma designs into the live CakePHP application using HTML and CSS. Made sure the implemented UI stayed consistent with the prototype across all user-facing pages.

Roles I wore on the team
Scoping is a real skill. Having honest conversations about what was achievable in the timeframe, without dismissing the client's vision, was one of the most valuable things I took from this project.
Good BA work makes development easier. When the ER diagram, user stories, and acceptance criteria were solid, the dev team had clear direction and there was far less back-and-forth each sprint.
Wearing multiple hats builds better empathy. Being the BA and the UI developer at the same time meant I felt the cost of my own decisions, which made me a much more precise writer of specs.
