Overview

What is CORE Transitions?

A Queensland-based disability services org supporting young adults through life skills, travel, and employment programs. When we got the brief, they had no digital platform. Participants couldn't view their goals, guardians had zero visibility, and everything ran on manual processes.

Our team Skellix was brought in to build a secure portal from scratch. I led the project as BA and Team Coordinator, while also owning the Figma prototype and frontend implementation.

The Challenge

Four very different users. One system.

The hard part wasn't the tech. It was designing. A participant needs something simple and encouraging. A guardian just needs to peek in. A support worker needs speed. An admin needs full control.

The client also came in with big ideas: gamification, badges, milestone rewards. Part of my job was having honest conversations about what was achievable in a semester, and documenting what stayed in scope vs what became future state.

What I Did

From first meeting to final handover

01
Business Vision
Ran discovery sessions and wrote the founding document: org background, system requirements, iteration priorities, timeline, team structure. Client signed off before a line of code was written.
02
Personas
Built personas for all 4 user types from what the client actually told us. These shaped every design decision, especially for participants, where accessibility mattered most.
03
Diagrams and Modelling
ER diagram, user journey maps, use case diagrams, functional decomposition. Shared reference for the whole team and fed directly into the database schema.
04
User Stories and Acceptance Criteria
Stories for all 4 roles across both iterations, each with acceptance criteria used as a definition of done in dev, and tested directly by the client in UAT.
05
Figma Prototype and Frontend Implementation
Designed all 4 dashboards in Figma: goal tracking, activity logging, shift notes, guardian views, admin management. Implemented in CakePHP with HTML and CSS.
06
Iteration Reports and UAT
Wrote iteration reports after each sprint. Ran UAT with the client, documented outcomes, and fed everything back into the next cycle.
07
User Training and Handover
Delivered a live Zoom walkthrough to the client team covering all 4 user roles. Then coordinated the full documentation and admin access handover.

Roles I wore on the team
Designing for accessibility sharpens your thinking. When users have varying disabilities and digital literacy, vague specs don't cut it. Every requirement had to be precise.
Good BA work is felt in development. Solid ER diagrams and user stories meant less back-and-forth and more building. Front-loaded effort pays off every sprint.
Being the BA and the developer is a superpower. Owning both sides meant I felt the impact of my own requirements, which made me write much better ones.
